Section 01

Overview

The core laws and facts of the chapter — no lecture, just what you need to hold in your head.

Basicity constant

RNH₂ + H₂O ⇌ RNH₃⁺ + OH⁻    Kb = [RNH₃⁺][OH⁻] / [RNH₂]
  • Amines are more basic than water, less basic than OH⁻.
  • Converted to salts by mineral acids; freed again by aqueous hydroxide.
Aliphatic: 10⁻³–10⁻⁴ Ammonia: 1.8×10⁻⁵ Aromatic: ≤10⁻⁹

Structure → basicity

  • The more stable the cation relative to the free amine, the more basic the amine.
  • N is less electronegative than O → better accommodates the + charge than alcohols/ethers.
  • Alkyl groups release electrons, disperse the cation's charge → aliphatic amines beat ammonia.
  • In aniline, the lone pair is delocalized into the ring → less available → weaker base.

Substituents on aniline

  • Electron-releasing (–NH₂, –OCH₃, –CH₃) → stabilize the cation → increase basicity.
  • Electron-withdrawing (–NH₃⁺, –NO₂, –SO₃⁻, –COOH, –X) → destabilize the cation → decrease basicity.

Amides from amines — quick rules

  • 1° amine + R'COCl → N-substituted amide (R'CONHR)
  • 2° amine + R'COCl → N,N-disubstituted amide (R'CONR₂)
  • 3° amine + R'COCl → no reaction (no N–H to lose as a proton)
  • Schotten–Baumann: acid chloride + amine, base = aqueous NaOH or pyridine.
